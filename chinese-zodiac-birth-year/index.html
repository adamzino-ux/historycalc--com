How It's Calculated

The Chinese zodiac is built from two overlapping cycles: 12 Earthly Branches (the animals) and 10 Heavenly Stems (five elements × Yin/Yang). Together they form a 60-year cycle where no animal-element combination repeats until 60 years have passed. The formulas, using the traditional reference point where the year 4 CE marks the start of the cycle, are:

Branch index (animal) = (Year − 4) mod 12
Stem index (element + polarity) = (Year − 4) mod 10

The 12 branch indices map to Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, Pig. The 10 stem indices map to Wood, Wood, Fire, Fire, Earth, Earth, Metal, Metal, Water, Water — with even indices assigned Yang and odd indices assigned Yin. If a birth date falls before that year's Chinese New Year (which moves between January 21 and February 20 depending on the lunar calendar), the effective zodiac year shifts back by one.

Worked Example

Take someone born on July 4, 1990. Since July is well after Chinese New Year, no adjustment is needed. Stem index = (1990 − 4) mod 10 = 1986 mod 10 = 6 → Metal, and 6 is even → Yang. Branch index = 1986 mod 12 = 6 → Horse. So this person is a Yang Metal Horse — matching the traditional name "Gengwu" year. Their best compatibility matches are Tiger and Dog (same trine group), while Rat is traditionally considered their most challenging match (the opposite sign, six positions away in the cycle).

Historical Context

The Earthly Branches were already used for day-counting on oracle bones during the Shang Dynasty (circa 1250 BCE), making this one of the oldest continuously used calendar systems in the world. The 12-animal zodiac as we know it was popularized during the Han Dynasty (206 BCE–220 CE); the earliest surviving text explicitly linking animals to branches is Wang Chong's 1st-century philosophical work "Lunheng." Folklore explains the animal order through the legend of the Jade Emperor's Great Race, where twelve animals crossed a river and finished in the order that still defines the cycle today. Notably, the 12-year length closely tracks Jupiter's roughly 11.86-year orbit — ancient astronomers tracked "Suixing" (the Year Star, i.e., Jupiter) moving through 12 sky sectors, which likely inspired the animal cycle's length.

Tips & Common Mistakes

• Don't assume your sign matches your plain birth year if you were born in January or early February — always check the exact Chinese New Year date for that year.
• Zodiac sign isn't destiny — full Chinese astrology ("Four Pillars of Destiny") also factors in your birth hour, day, and month animals, not just the year.
• Element and animal combinations repeat every 60 years, so grandparents and grandchildren often share identical signs.
• Compatibility charts are cultural tradition, not scientific fact — enjoy them as insight, not as hard relationship rules.
• "Goat," "Sheep," and "Ram" all refer to the same 8th zodiac sign — regional translations vary, but the animal is identical.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is my Chinese zodiac if I was born in January?

If you were born before that year's Chinese New Year (which falls between January 21 and February 20), your zodiac animal is actually the previous year's sign. Enter your exact birth month and day above for an automatic adjustment based on real Chinese New Year dates.

How many Chinese zodiac animals are there?

There are 12 animals in the cycle: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig. Each pairs with one of five elements over a 60-year cycle, so no combination repeats for six decades.

What are the 5 elements in Chinese astrology?

The five elements are Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water. Combined with Yin and Yang polarity they create 10 unique "stem" combinations that pair with the 12 animals to form the full 60-year sexagenary cycle.

Which zodiac signs are most compatible?

Animals within the same "trine" group are considered most compatible: Rat-Dragon-Monkey, Ox-Snake-Rooster, Tiger-Horse-Dog, and Rabbit-Goat-Pig. Animals directly opposite each other in the cycle, six positions apart, are traditionally seen as the most challenging matches.

Is the Chinese zodiac the same as Western astrology?

No. Western astrology is based on the sun's position and changes monthly, while the Chinese zodiac assigns one animal sign per year, tied to a 12-year cycle plus a 10-year elemental cycle from the lunar calendar.

What year is the next Year of the Dragon?

After 2024's Wood Dragon year, the next Dragon year will be 2036, expected to be a Fire Dragon, since each animal sign returns every 12 years.
